🧠 About Minds

Nowadays, there are two kinds of minds: biological and machine.

For example:

  • Biological minds are the brains of humans and other animals.
  • Machine minds are AI systems — the most powerful of which are called Transformers, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Grok.

The main job of every mind — whether biological or machine — is to learn and think.

And here’s something surprising: at their core, learning and thinking are actually just doing math. Yes, really!

Math happens in every kind of mind, whether biological or machine, although through different means.

When a mind is created, it learns on its own. So creating it is more like training or growing something than building a LEGO house.

How is all this even possible? In this course, you’ll discover the answer.

The Building Blocks of Every Mind

Every mind is, in essence, a NEURAL NETWORK.

It’s called "NEURAL" because it consists of NEURONS.

😎
NEURON

And it’s called "NETWORK" because the NEURONS connect to each other and work together.

We can think of each NEURON as a tiny yet cool creation.

NEURONS are like tiny helpers that learn and think on their own, helping the whole mind get smarter.

But what does every NEURON do?

Actually, every NEURON takes in input SIGNALS and then sends out one output SIGNAL.

For example, a NEURON might take in two input SIGNALS and then send out one output SIGNAL:

What is a SIGNAL?

Generally, a SIGNAL is just anything — like an electric impulse — that can be measured or calculated and then represented as a number. For example: 0.36 or 0.53.

At this point, we don't need to worry about how SIGNALS are measured or calculated. We just need to remember that:

Every SIGNAL is something that can be represented as a number.

In more detail, every NEURON:

  • Takes in input SIGNALS (say, 0.36 and 0.53)
  • Does simple math on them to calculate its output SIGNAL (say, 0.79)
  • Then sends out this output SIGNAL

  • The input SIGNALS are called INPUTS.

The Math Every Neuron Does

When a mind thinks, every NEURON does very simple math.

At its core, every NEURON works just like a store receipt.

Analogy: RECEIPT

Imagine buying donuts and cupcakes at a store:

RECEIPT
UNITS PRICE PER UNIT AMOUNT
donut 4 2.00 8.00
cupcake 2 3.00 6.00
SUM 14.00
DISCOUNT -0.50
TOTAL 13.50

The math is simple:

TOTAL = 4.00 × 2.00 + 2.00 × 3.00 + (-0.50)
TOTAL = 8.00 + 6.00 - 0.50
TOTAL = 14.00 - 0.50
TOTAL = 13.50

This math can also be represented this way:

4.002.00UNITS
2.003.00PRICES
8.006.00AMOUNTS
14.00SUM
-0.50DISCOUNT
13.50TOTAL

At its core, every RECEIPT is just a formula:

TOTAL = UNITS1 × PRICE1 + UNITS2 × PRICE2 + DISCOUNT
TOTAL = 4.00 × 2.00 + 2.00 × 3.00 + (-0.50)
TOTAL = 8.00 + 6.00 - 0.50
TOTAL = 14.00 - 0.50
TOTAL = 13.50

In terms of a NEURON, the same math can be represented this way:

4.002.00INPUTS
2.003.00WEIGHTS
8.006.00PRODUCTS
14.00SUM
-0.50BIAS
13.50OUTPUT

At its core, every NEURON is also just a formula:

OUTPUT = INPUT1 × WEIGHT1 + INPUT2 × WEIGHT2 + BIAS
OUTPUT = 4.00 × 2.00 + 2.00 × 3.00 + (-0.50)
OUTPUT = 8.00 + 6.00 - 0.50
OUTPUT = 14.00 - 0.50
OUTPUT = 13.50

Ultra simple. No math changed. Only names changed.

Importantly, in the case of RECEIPTS, when UNITS change, the TOTAL changes too:

3.005.00INPUTS
2.003.00WEIGHTS
6.0015.00PRODUCTS
21.00SUM
-0.50BIAS
20.50OUTPUT

Again, no math changed. Only names changed.

So every NEURON follows the same basic logic as a RECEIPT.

The mapping:

RECEIPT → NEURON
UNITS→INPUTS
PRICES→WEIGHTS
AMOUNTS→PRODUCTS
SUM→SUM
DISCOUNT→BIAS
TOTAL→OUTPUT

The WEIGHTS and BIAS are called PARAMETERS.

If you understand this, you already understand a lot of AI.

By the way, every answer from ChatGPT ultimately comes from billions of tiny calculations that look surprisingly similar to the receipt example above.

The most exciting part is how NEURONS themselves improve their PARAMETERS after making ERRORS. This process is called LEARNING.

Scale: Billions and Trillions

While today's largest AI models may each have over 1 trillion PARAMETERS, the human brain is vastly more complex.

A typical human brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons with up to 500 trillion neural connections called synapses. You can think of each synapse as playing the role of a WEIGHT in an artificial neural network, while each biological neuron also has properties that loosely resemble a BIAS.

Metaphorically speaking, a typical human brain has roughly 500.86 trillion "parameters" (500 trillion "weights" + 86 billion "biases").

So every individual NEURON is simple, but large networks of NEURONS can produce remarkably intelligent behavior.

🌺 Our Very First Machine Mind

Moreover, this NEURON will only have a single PARAMETER: a single WEIGHT — no other WEIGHTS and no BIAS. A single WEIGHT. That’s it.

This tiniest-ever mind will first learn from its own ERRORS. Then, after LEARNING, it will be able to think — solving problems it has never encountered before.

Indeed, this is the essence of every genuine mind: solving problems it has never encountered before.
